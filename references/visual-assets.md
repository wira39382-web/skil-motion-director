# Visual assets and element craft

## Make a cast of visual subjects

Treat the important elements as a designed cast, not a collection of interchangeable icons. Assign roles: focal subject, interacting/supporting objects, environment, typography, and accents. These are roles, not a minimum object count. Give the focal subject a recognizable silhouette and an action tied to the argument. A small symbol may be sufficient for a label; an enlarged stock icon rarely sustains a hero shot by itself.

For every focal subject record a compact asset specification: narrative role; silhouette/geometry; medium; material and light if relevant; distinctive detail; separable moving parts; interaction; small-screen limits; provenance. Include only fields that help production. Do not require the user to supply these decisions.

Example specification: **server cluster** → conveys concentrated computing capacity; staggered rack silhouettes, matte dark metal, restrained edge light, coherent perspective; trays/lights/cable routes on separate layers; incoming resources activate one cluster while another lacks a connection. It is a conceptual illustration, not a measured operational dashboard. Do not reuse this metaphor for every technology topic.

## Choose the right construction medium

| Need | Medium to consider | Craft to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explain a mechanism or transform | Original layered SVG/vector | Clear silhouette, separate parts, deliberate geometry and origins |
| Express character, atmosphere or a distinctive world | Original illustration, including image generation when available | Consistent perspective, palette, character and scene lighting |
| Ground a real entity or event | Supplied/licensed photography or documented footage | Identity, provenance, crop quality and honest context |
| Show volume or a spatial relationship | Layered 2.5D or justified 3D | Coherent camera, scale, contact, lighting and render budget |
| Make a statement through language | Custom typographic composition | Hierarchy, rhythm, spacing and semantic emphasis |

Select by the story, available tools and delivery budget. Flat vector work can be excellent; depth, realism and 3D are not automatic upgrades. Use imagegen through its skill for appropriate raster assets; build exact diagrams, data, text, logos and vector geometry through suitable deterministic tools. Do not promise access to asset services. Resolve rights and brand constraints, and do useful work with authorized assets already available.

## Establish one visual language

- **Shape:** choose a coherent family of proportions, corner treatment, stroke weight and perspective. Let a contrasting shape signal a contrasting role.
- **Surface:** choose flat ink, paper, glass, metal, clay or another justified treatment. Do not combine arbitrary glossy icons, outline symbols and unrelated photo cutouts without a deliberate collage direction.
- **Light:** when volume is used, establish a source direction, edge behavior and contact shadows. Objects touching a surface should feel grounded; a shadow must not drift independently of its object.
- **Detail:** put meaningful detail where the camera gets close. Keep distant/supporting objects simpler. Avoid decorative microtext, invented control panels, and technical-looking noise that suggests nonexistent evidence.
- **Color:** allocate contrast and saturation by narrative role. Keep the strongest accent available for emphasis; do not make every object equally bright.
- **Type:** use typographic scale, cropping, line breaks and spacing deliberately. Kinetic type can interact with objects or become a diagram, but preserve readable words and factual meaning.

Prefer a few well-built elements to many loosely related ones. Do not ban simple circles, lines or rectangles: combine and choreograph them purposefully. Reject generic appearance based on the composition and behavior, not a blacklist of primitive shapes or fonts.

## Build for movement

Design the final readable pose and at least one useful changed state before writing entrance tweens. Identify moving subparts, contact points, occlusion and the next shot's handoff. Separate body, surface detail, shadow, highlight and annotations only where they need independent behavior; do not overengineer a still object.

For SVG, keep viewBox and group origins explicit, avoid clipped strokes, and expose IDs for purposeful parts. Keep text editable when legibility matters. For raster cutouts, inspect edges and resolution at the intended crop. Do not claim a single flattened image has independently animated parts. Repeated redraws or generated variants must preserve recognizable object identity unless transformation is the point.

Use details as actions: a hinge opens, a paper strip unfolds, a connection completes, layers separate to reveal structure. A highlight crossing a static icon is finishing, not the main explanatory action. Make secondary motion follow the main action instead of competing with it.

## Develop before multiplying

Combine the asset study with the existing three-keyframe and short-motion prototype; do not add another user approval gate. Inspect the main subject at its largest crop and in a complete phone-size frame. Check silhouette, craft, style consistency, relationship to type, and what the object can actually do. If it looks generic or unresolved, redesign that subject before duplicating it through the film.

Show visual richness through hierarchy, varying scale, overlap, selective detail and controlled contrast. Filling empty areas with particles or turning every object into a glowing card does not repair a weak subject. A quiet composition can still feel authored and complete.

## Diagnostic examples

| Brief | Underdeveloped treatment | More specific element treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI investment and access | Chip icon, arrows, repeated title cards | A layered computing cluster whose connections/activation reveal access differences; retain accurate data separately |
| Cheap purchases becoming expensive | Shopping-cart icon plus a red number | A receipt that unfolds as additional costs appear, with the supporting object changing under its weight; label illustrative costs unless real data are supplied |
| A good idea failing in execution | Lightbulb icon beside three bullets | A designed object with identifiable parts that cannot engage until one missing connection is built; give that repair a satisfying readable action |

These are examples of specificity, not mandatory metaphors. Create a new treatment when the topic or requested style calls for it.

## Asset review

Mark each focal asset pass / needs revision / not checked, with an observed reason. Critical defects include identity distortion, unusable cutout edges, illegible detail, broken perspective/contact, inconsistent lighting, missing dependencies and an object that cannot perform its planned action. Judge the full frame and movement as well as the isolated asset. Geometry validation alone cannot prove aesthetic quality. If visual inspection is unavailable, state that limit; do not describe the asset as professionally validated.
