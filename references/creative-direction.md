# Creative direction

## Translate “keren” into decisions
Choose a visual thesis: one sentence describing how the viewer will understand the idea through objects and changes. Example: repeated advertisements gradually crowd one person's field of view; relief reveals what remains in memory. This is a conceptual illustration, not measured research data.

Make the opening immediately legible: one dominant subject, a meaningful contrast/question, and a purposeful change. Do not default to floating titles, three feature cards, then a logo. A restrained opening can be powerful; do not require an effect every second.

Choose one composition system and one motion signature. Specify actual palette roles, font roles, light/material, hierarchy and spatial relationships. Keep accent color for meaning. Prefer two font families maximum; any exception must serve the concept. Keep primary text concise and check it at phone size. Source credits must remain readable without overpowering the narrative.

## Three families
- Editorial: evidence, scale, context, contrast. Use documents, maps from verified sources, annotations and meaningful image crops. Documentary footage and AI illustration must be distinguishable. Calm camera and decisive cuts can coexist.
- Illustrated: show mechanism and causality through changing states. Maintain character/object consistency. Use diagrams when more accurate than characters. Physical actions must match the narrated process.
- Product: preserve real product geometry, logo and claims. Show a problem, an actual product action, and the resulting benefit. Without supplied evidence, describe intended features as such. Avoid fictional dashboards presented as operational product features.

## Design every beat
For each beat answer: what does the viewer learn; what is dominant; what changes; what remains continuous; why this transition? Replace descriptive decoration with visual explanation. Use a brief hold when text/data needs reading. A “hero moment” is optional and must express the message, not interrupt it.

Use contrast in scale, density, tempo and framing. Never maximize all four together. Build a rhythm of establish → develop → emphasize → release, adapted to content rather than repeated every scene.

## Quality references
If user supplies a reference, extract pacing, hierarchy, framing and material treatment. Reuse authorized brand assets only. With no reference, confidently choose a direction and explain it briefly; do not ask the user to become an art director.

## Natural revisions
“Lebih keren” → inspect whether the issue is generic concept, weak hierarchy, poor assets, stiff motion or flat pacing; name the main issue and fix it.
“Lebih cinematic” → improve framing, continuity, depth, lighting and pacing; do not automatically darken everything.
“Lebih ramai” → increase meaningful activity while preserving a dominant subject.
“Lebih premium” → refine spacing, typography, restraint, materials and timing; do not default to gold.
“Jangan ubah gaya” → freeze tokens and motion signature; change only requested content/composition.

## Acceptance before polishing
Write a short internal direction card: main idea; visual metaphor; three style attributes; palette roles; type roles; focal object; movement signature; one thing deliberately omitted. It must explain this topic specifically. If changing the topic word leaves the concept unchanged, redesign it.

For a 45–60 second explainer do not generate a fixed scene count: allow several related phrases within one evolving scene. Use different shot scales only when useful. Give opening, explanation and ending distinct visual functions without forcing a repeated template.

Do not replace all supplied assets with generated illustrations just for consistency. Preserve accurate product images and documentary evidence. Missing premium assets should lead to an intentional graphic solution, never fake screenshots or fabricated brand features.
