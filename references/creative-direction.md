# Creative direction

## Translate “keren” into decisions
Choose a visual thesis: one sentence describing how the viewer will understand the idea through objects and changes. Example: repeated advertisements gradually crowd one person's field of view; relief reveals what remains in memory. This is a conceptual illustration, not measured research data.

Make the opening immediately legible: one dominant subject, a meaningful contrast/question, and a purposeful change. Do not default to floating titles, three feature cards, then a logo. A restrained opening can be powerful; do not require an effect every second.

Choose one composition system and one motion signature. Specify actual palette roles, font roles, light/material, hierarchy and spatial relationships. Keep accent color for meaning. Prefer two font families maximum; any exception must serve the concept. Keep primary text concise and check it at phone size. Source credits must remain readable without overpowering the narrative.

## Art direction contract
Before coding, make these choices explicit in the project record: visual thesis; dominant subject; motif carried between beats; palette roles; typography roles; asset medium; light/material; composition rhythm; motion signature; audio character. A palette plus two fonts is not a complete direction.

Choose the medium that expresses the idea: original vector scene, expressive illustration, licensed photo collage, tactile editorial treatment, or purposeful 2.5D/3D. Use available image-generation tools for original illustration/textures when that materially improves the story, following their skill; do not generate charts, logos or precise data. If assets cannot be obtained, simplify the concept deliberately rather than leaving generic placeholders. Never assume all topics should look like a technology dashboard.

Choose one or two related visual anchors tailored to the subject, with room to transform. For AI investment, a chip alone says “technology”; showing how resources gather around infrastructure and access barriers explains distribution. A subject should do something, not merely sit below a heading.

Use the element-craft guidance in visual-assets.md, linked directly from SKILL.md, to turn the anchor into a production-ready subject. Specify its silhouette, meaningful details, medium, changed states and interaction with surrounding objects/type. Examine the resulting artwork; a written specification does not prove that the asset has been made well.

Plan contrasting compositions: close detail, wider relationship, data emphasis, quiet payoff, as appropriate. Do not repeat a top headline + centered icon + bottom paragraph for the whole film. Keep coherent type, margins, materials and colors while changing scale and relationships. Avoid repeated rounded cards or full-screen numbered lists as a substitute for art direction.

Use a dominant visual region and secondary text region; allow an object to cross those boundaries for a reason. Balance empty space against a strong subject. “Minimal” must not mean sparse because no assets were made. Keep source credits secondary and the detailed evidence available outside the cinematic canvas where useful. At a 390-pixel-wide review viewport, check readability and text overlap; use that viewport as a check, not a guarantee for every platform overlay.

## Three families
- Editorial: evidence, scale, context, contrast. Use documents, maps from verified sources, annotations and meaningful image crops. Documentary footage and AI illustration must be distinguishable. Calm camera and decisive cuts can coexist.
- Illustrated: show mechanism and causality through changing states. Maintain character/object consistency. Use diagrams when more accurate than characters. Physical actions must match the narrated process.
- Product: preserve real product geometry, logo and claims. Show a problem, an actual product action, and the resulting benefit. Without supplied evidence, describe intended features as such. Avoid fictional dashboards presented as operational product features.

## Design every beat
For each beat answer: what does the viewer learn; what is dominant; what changes; what remains continuous; why this transition? Replace descriptive decoration with visual explanation. Use a brief hold when text/data needs reading. Build a memorable visual payoff that expresses the message; its form can be quiet or dramatic. Do not interrupt the explanation with an unrelated spectacle.

Use contrast in scale, density, tempo and framing. Never maximize all four together. Build a rhythm of establish → develop → emphasize → release, adapted to content rather than repeated every scene.

## Quality references
If user supplies a reference, extract pacing, hierarchy, framing and material treatment. Reuse authorized brand assets only. With no reference, confidently choose a direction and explain it briefly; do not ask the user to become an art director.

## Natural revisions
“Lebih keren” → inspect whether the issue is generic concept, weak hierarchy, poor assets, stiff motion or flat pacing; name the main issue and fix it.
“Lebih cinematic” → improve framing, continuity, depth, lighting and pacing; do not automatically darken everything.
“Lebih ramai” → increase meaningful activity while preserving a dominant subject.
“Lebih premium” → refine spacing, typography, restraint, materials and timing; do not default to gold.
“Jangan ubah gaya” → freeze tokens and motion signature; change only requested content/composition.

## Concrete benchmark

Weak: seven identically framed headlines, each sliding upward above an icon, with a new background at every cut.
Stronger: establish a meaningful object, follow its transformation through contrasting compositions, let the main data point interrupt the rhythm, then resolve the same motif in a clear payoff. Labels help identify; voice explains; motion reveals the relationship. This is a diagnostic comparison, not a mandatory story recipe.

If the chosen direction could fit any topic after swapping text and one icon, redesign its visual thesis before production. Assess appeal through inspected frames and movement, not adjectives in the brief.
